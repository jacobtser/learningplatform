01 The problem Low-resource

Low-resource is not a description of the speakers. It is a description of the data.

Dangme is a Kwa language of the Ga-Dangme family, spoken across Ghana’s Greater Accra and Eastern coastal plains. Around 1.4 million people speak it. In software, it is close to absent.

A language with a million voices, barely present online.

  1. 01

    Thin digital ground

    Little digital learning material exists, reliable machine translation does not, and text corpora are small. A spoken language of over a million people has almost no footprint in the tools children now learn from.

  2. 02

    No commercial pull

    There is no strong commercial incentive for large vendors to build for a language this size. When no one is obliged to serve a language, it waits quietly for someone who cares enough to try.

  3. 03

    English-first by default

    Children who speak Dangme at home are handed English-first tools that say, without meaning to, that their first language is not for screens. Learning should start in the language a child already thinks in.

02 The platform Built & shipping

What already runs today.

This is not a proposal. The platform is built and working: three front-ends over one untouched Flask backend, a fine-tuned translation model and Dangme speech — all running on a laptop, offline-first.

Flask SQLAlchemy · SQLite MarianMT PWA · offline-first CSRF · rate-limited

01

Translator — EN ⇄ Dangme

A fine-tuned MarianMT model translates in both directions, served through a thread-safe inference API, with suggestions and a contribution route back into the lexicon.

02

Dangme text-to-speech

Any word or phrase can be spoken aloud, and pronunciation practice is built into the games — so a learner hears the tone and rhythm, not only the spelling.

03

26 learning games

Vocabulary, numbers, maths-in-Dangme, currency, time and seasons, boggle, crossword, memory, hangman, scramble, word-ladder, minimal pairs and more — authored as real lessons.

04

Spaced-repetition study

SM-2 flashcards schedule each review at the moment of forgetting, with progress tracking that follows individual learners across sessions.

05

Teacher dashboard + leaderboard

Teachers see class progress and export results to CSV; learners share a leaderboard that turns daily practice into a habit worth keeping.

06

Offline-first PWA

Built to run locally and to survive intermittent connectivity, with a service worker — a deliberate choice for the contexts the platform actually serves.

03 Why credits $1,000 · one quarter

Compute and tokens are the bottleneck — not ideas, not engineering.

For a low-resource language, the dataset is small — and hand-authoring does not scale.

The bilingual lexicon holds roughly 13,000 Dangme→English and 14,000 English→Dangme entries, plus the sentence pairs in the corpus. That is a real foundation, and still far short of what a language needs.

Authoring curriculum, distractor sets, grammar tables and pronunciation review by hand cannot keep pace with 26 game modes. And our in-app AI tutor runs on free tiers today, so it fails exactly when a learner needs it most.

Credits turn that bottleneck into throughput. Every line of the plan below maps to something we can build, verify and publish.

The $1,000 credit plan
# Workstream & delivery Credits
01 Curriculum & content generation Graded lesson sets, example sentences and distractor pools generated and reviewed for all 26 game modes — turning a hand-written library into a scalable one. $350
02 Parallel-data expansion & post-editing LLM-assisted back-translation and post-editing to grow the ~13k bilingual lexicon, plus synthetic augmentation for scarce sentence types. $250
03 Translation quality evaluation LLM-as-judge scoring across a held-out EN ⇄ Dangme test set, alongside BLEU and chrF, to find and fix the model’s real failure modes. $150
04 In-app Dangme tutor (beta) Keep the provider-agnostic conversational tutor online for a pilot cohort — no more free-tier outages mid-lesson. $150
05 Speech data prep & pronunciation QA Transcription, tone-mark checking and pronunciation QA for the text-to-speech and pronunciation features. $100
Total One quarter of focused, shippable AI work. $1,000

Provider-agnostic by design. The tutor endpoint already speaks to multiple providers, so whichever company grants the credits, the work runs on their models. And what we build with them — an expanded corpus, grammar tables and evaluation reports — will be published openly, for this language and for the ones that follow.

05 Impact Beyond the product

Why it matters beyond the product.

The credits do more than improve one app. They help a language stay spoken, help children learn to read, and leave open resources behind for others.

Language preservation

A language lives in its daily use, not in its archive. Putting Dangme into a translator, a classroom and a child’s practice is how it stays spoken rather than simply remembered.

Mother-tongue literacy

Children learn to read faster in the language they already think in. Dangme-first material meets a learner where they are before it carries them into English.

Open resources

The expanded corpus, grammar tables and evaluation reports will be published, so the next low-resource language starts from further ahead than Dangme did.
